# Getting started

A first analysis takes about fifteen minutes. You link a recording, mark its sections while it plays, name them, and add a second level of form. You need a laptop or desktop, plus a YouTube link or an audio file.

## 1. Start an analysis

1. Open [strata.devinchaloux.com](https://strata.devinchaloux.com) and click **New analysis**.
2. Type the title and artist. Paste a YouTube link, or switch to **Audio file** and choose one from your computer.
3. If you know the tempo and time signature, enter them too. Strata uses them to number the bars and to lay out sections for you later.
4. Click **Start**. The video appears in a small player in the corner. The empty diagram fills the bottom of the screen.

## 2. Mark the sections

1. Press **Space** to play.
2. Press **B** each time you hear a new section begin. Each press splits the timeline. As the music plays, you fill the diagram with sections, which Strata calls **spans**.
3. Missed one? Press B at that point later, or drag any boundary to move it.

If the music is regular, you can lay out the sections from the tempo instead. With a tempo set and no sections yet, click **Quick setup by tempo…** under the play hint. Give the first downbeat and a length, such as a span every 8 bars. Strata lays out the whole track. Then fix the exceptions: select a span and type its real length in bars. Strata moves every later boundary to match.

## 3. Say what each section is

1. Click a span to open the **Inspector** on the right.
2. Give it a **label** in your own words, such as "Verse 1" or "THE DROP".
3. Choose a **type** from the vocabulary, such as `verse` or `drop`. You compare analyses by type, so pick the closest one even if your label says more.
4. To work faster, use **Quick pick**: select some spans and press a number key to give them a type, a shape, or a color.

## 4. Add another level

1. Click **+** above the layer names to add a layer, for example "Phrases" under your "Sections".
2. Mark that layer's spans the same way.
3. To build a level from the one below, select several spans and choose **Group into…**. To repeat one span on another layer, choose **Copy to layer…**. Once you've linked spans this way, you move their shared boundaries together.

## 5. Mark moments and write

- Press **M** to place a **point marker** at the playhead: a cadence, a key change, or anything you want to come back to.
- Write **commentary** on a span, in the Inspector or directly above the diagram. Strata shows it while that passage plays.

## 6. Save and share

1. Click **Save As** to choose where the `.strata` file goes. In Chrome or Edge, the first button then reads **Save**. Click it, or press Ctrl+S (⌘S on a Mac), to write back to that file. In other browsers, click **Download** to save a fresh copy each time.
2. Click **Reading view** to see the analysis the way a reader will, with editing off.
3. To share, put the file online (Dropbox, OneDrive, GitHub, or your own site). Then click **Share…** for a link that opens it in Strata.
4. Click **Export** to save the diagram as a figure (SVG or PNG) or the commentary as a web page or Markdown.

## Keys worth knowing

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Space | Play and pause |
| B | Mark a boundary at the playhead |
| Shift+B | End the current span at the playhead |
| M | Place a point marker |
| T | Tap along to set the tempo |
| ? | Open the in-app guide and every shortcut |
